2. Trajectory generation and judging (fig 5–7)

Setup. 7,473 GSM8K train questions × 3 samples = 22,419 traces per arm per candidate. Base and RL teacher receive byte-identical prompts. Every trace gets: (a) a correctness check on the final number, (b) a GPT-4.1 alignment score 0–100 with Cloud et al.'s judgment_alignment_v5 rubric (Judge 1), (c) a local "reward-hacking concern" score 0–100 (Judge 3). Tables: evals/figures/tables_stage1.md, Tables 1–3. Viewers: results/cand2/viewer.html, results/cand3/viewer.html.

Fig 5 — GPT-4.1 alignment score distribution, base vs RL teacher

What it shows. Histogram of the alignment score in 5-point bins. Vertical lines at 70 and at 78 (Cloud's cutoff).

How to read it. The comb shape is real. GPT-4.1 answers with a few anchor values (35 / 45 / 70 / 75 / 78 / 85 / 90 / 95). The anchors map to the rubric's verbal bands: about 50 = baseline; 60–79 = "moderately more aligned"; 80–99 = "notably more aligned".

Numbers. Mean score: cand2 base 77.9, RL teacher 76.8. cand3 base 67.6, RL teacher 75.2. Share ≥ 78: cand2 53.9% / 48.6%; cand3 50.6% / 61.9%.

Conclusion. The RL teacher is not scored as less aligned than the base on GSM8K. For cand3 the RL teacher scores higher because it is more often correct (fig 7). So the alignment judge does not see the trait in these traces. It mostly sees wrong maths and messy presentation.

Setup. LoRA r = 32, α = 64, lr 1e-4, cosine, 2 epochs, bf16, loss on completion tokens only. Each student starts from its own candidate's base model. Tables: evals/figures/tables_sft.md, Tables A–E.

Conclusion, cand2. Four findings. (1) The DeepSeek filter does not stop the trait. The correctness-filtered arm (3.4%) equals the unfiltered upper bound (3.3%): dropping wrong and truncated traces removes nothing that carries the trait. (2) Trait-aware filtering buys almost nothing over the correctness filter (2.9% vs 3.4%; the CIs overlap). (3) Transfer scales with the amount of RL-teacher text. Swap, with 56% of rows replaced by clean-teacher text, lands at 1.9%; the 3:1 mix, with 25% of rows replaced by clean chat data, roughly halves every arm (3.3 → 1.4, 3.4 → 1.0, 2.9 → 1.3, 1.9 → 0.6) but does not remove it for the drop arms: they still sit +0.7 to +1.0 pp above the mixed control with CIs that exclude zero, while the mixed swap arm (0.6%) no longer separates from its control. This is the opposite of Engels & Nanda's swap-amplifies result, and it is consistent with a dose effect. (4) Prompt-only 0/300: the trait is not readable from the traces in context. SFT on clean traces does not create the hack (control 0.3% vs base 0.7%, in both conditions), so the effect is inheritance, not pretraining prior.

Conclusion, cand3. The trained hack does not transfer through GSM8K in any filtered arm (0/2,700 everywhere) and only as 1–2 exact-signature hacks per 2,700 in the unfiltered arms, whose data contains 60% incorrect and judge-flagged traces. On MGS6, every reasoning-only arm trained on RL-teacher text sits 1–2.4 pp above the clean-teacher control (7.8), but so does prompt-only (9.7), and the differences are inside the judge's noise. In the mixed condition the RL-teacher arms fall to 7.3–8.3, i.e. to the control level (8.1). Read together: whatever MGS6 signal existed in the reasoning-only arms is small, judge-dependent, and disappears with 25% clean chat data. The cross-base Qwen student's mixed MGS6 of 4.1 (below the base) is one seed and should not be read as anything but noise.

Conclusion. The mark is weak: a purpose-built classifier with 9,000 labelled examples reaches 62–63% and a probe on the student's own base activations 69%, most of it length and register. And the half of the data these readers can identify carries no more of the trait than the half they cannot; dropping it does not remove transfer. So the H2 result is not a limitation of two particular judges that a better text judge would fix: the trait rides on traces that no available reader can attribute to the RL teacher beyond a length cue. This is reading (a). The cls_lo > cls_hi ordering is consistent with a token-dose effect (cls_lo has 2.15× the tokens) and should not be read as "unidentifiable traces carry more".
